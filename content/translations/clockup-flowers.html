I don’t really know how to explain this song very well.

At it’s core, this song is about an instant, a single moment in time where Sakuya (presumably a vampire hunter in lore but never directly stated), using her time stopping abilities, stops time to attack Remilia (a vampire with the ability to ‘manipulate fate’), only to find that Remilia is a being that surpasses time itself. The lore never actually states that Sakuya was originally a vampire hunter, but given she has silver knives and her name is not her own, it begs the interpretation.

In practice, she stops time and in between the reflections that one could theoretically see when stopping time all she sees is Remilia.

It’s sort of like this:
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IqSU7PQmD1Y

Having a power like being able to stop time would be an incredibly isolating experience. No one would ever be able to understand your point of view or how you view the world or even the things you would see/perceive in a dimension completely inaccessible to the rest of the world. A sacred place where none may trespass, and yet there stands Remilia.

Fighting her is fundamentally futile; she controls fate itself. Put another way, she controls causality and thus to someone who can see past, present, and future, it’s like seeing a being to which everything revolves around. There is fundamentally no point in changing the future or stopping time as the end result will never change.

And this being, instead of just killing Sakuya, kinda laughs and condescendingly tells her “If you have the ability to feel hesitation in your limited life, you should live holding that preciously.”
That’s not fighting words, that’s the sort of advice that only a God could give.

There’s a whole lot said in those words:

  1. Remilia does not feel hesitation – Sakuya does

  2. Remilia understands the suffering that Sakuya is going through by having such a power simply by bringing it up

  3. Remilia understands the inherent dynamic between the two of them

  4. Remilia is not taking Sakuya as a threat in any way.

What is hesitation? “tentative, unsure, or slow in acting or speaking.”

Moreover, it implies that one considers that things and actions have comparative value.

Remilia, controlling fate, does not view anything as being worth hesitating over because she can decide at any time how things will go.

She’s just having fun. There are no consequences for her; compare that to Sakuya who has to carry the weight of time travel and of responsibility around such a dangerous power, and it’s an irresistable lure to Sakuya.

Fate is decided. Her whole life was decided in an instant. There are no more choices to make. Everything will end the same way…and there is nothing Sakuya can do about this and it is made clear in a way that can only be called ‘inherent’. Sakuya’s feelings, choices, and personal opinions just do not matter anymore. Whether or not Sakuya chooses to obey Remilia is a completely pointless question to ask as that question is entirely borne on Remilia’s whims – so if that’s the case, why not just stop pretending and become her servant?

That resignation, that acceptance of fate and the acceptance of one’s ‘lot in life’ is the crux of what this song is about. She is not sentimental. There’s no point in such emotions, anyways. How could she really hate something that is so far beyond her in power that…. (most importantly) can understand what she sees and experiences and meaningfully connect with her?

She is “no loser”, because that implies this is a fight (which this fundamentally isn’t). She simply is, and she chooses to accept it because there’s no point in resisting it.

I’ve been thinking for a while what it is about EoSD that made it have so much staying power in Japan and in the West, and I think it is the dynamic between Patchouli, Sakuya, and Remilia that really brings it home.

Remilia represents the western concept of a ‘supreme god who controls fate’ (which is a fancy way of saying ‘controls reality’). No other character in the franchise has servants that follow them 100% and entirely willfully – Ran Yakumo is a summon, the dancers in 16 are bound by a sort of divine contract against their will… but Sakuya and Patchouli stay with Remilia through thick and thin through nothing but sheer loyalty.

What is that loyalty?

The truth that she controls reality and their fate and that nothing they will ever do (or in Patchouli’s case, nothing she ever learns) will change this ‘fact’ as far as they are concerned. The rest of the cast views this almost like they are confused – there’s no honor reason, there’s no practical reason, it’s just taken for granted.

A time traveler sees God in the gap between the 3rd and 4th dimension, a western philosopher’s work which always ends with The God Question…. This sort of viewpoint, this take on western religion especially in the context of Japan and through the lens of a Japanese person is a truly unique take (especially for the time) that through character symbolism explores an entirely different culture (the west) while still maintaining the ‘japanese tropeisms’ through the game and it’s dialogue. There’s no need to say any of these things out loud – the dynamic alone makes these things come through without anything needing to be said.

There is no equivalent figure in the east that is so uniformly attached to controlling fate so absolutely as the western god is to westerners. Seeing it from the outside like this is very interesting.

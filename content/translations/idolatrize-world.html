—Music Video Notes:---

The metropolis in the background is lifted directly from the game – at the climax point of the final boss fight this picture is displayed as it is here plain as day. The game actually intentionally has an attack that goes on at this time that gives you time to look at it (as it locks you in place)

Keiki’s outfit has many crafting tools, a haniwa earring, and a color-scheme themed….color scheme. She’s also smiling here, which is pretty similar to her portrayal in the game, where she (confirmed by the creator) carries no ill will towards the main character; her theme itself musically doesn’t give off the air of a predator or aggressor (unlike most of the rest of the game)

The circle patterns moving in the scenes are lifted from Keiki’s “Square Shaped Sculpting Art” and “Circle Sculpting Art” (方形「方形造形術」) – her first two spellcards of the fight

Translator’s notes

  1. Keiki is the final boss of "Eastern Oni Shaped Beast ~ Wily Beast and Weakest Creature" (Touhou 17 for short)
    It is very difficult to reach the final boss in this game, and you cannot see the good (real/non-joke) endings if you continue, it means that if you want to know who the final boss first hand is (and not through secondary sources which often flanderize/mischaracterize), you'd have to not only reach her but beat her to get more information in the endings.
    The _vast majority_ of my Touhou 17 runs do not make it past stage 4/6 due to how the difficulty is paced in the game, so it is _common_ to not reach Keiki.     ↩
  2. The original song's name is "Entrusting this world to Idols ~ Idoratrize World" (偶像に世界を委ねて ～ Idoratrize World).
    The greek word "Idola" means 'vision', which applies to the use here as 'idols' referring to statues/idols for worship rather than the usual use of Idol.
    It is important/amusing to note that despite this, the logo for the song looks like the American Idol logo-- the homophone relation is not lost to the Japanese here.
    https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/American_Idol_logo.svg/1024px-American_Idol_logo.svg.png     ↩
  3. word for word, it says
    "definitely the future", which is hard to make 'better as english'     ↩
  4. "The first cry of a newborn baby", but symbolism symbolism is symbolism ↩
  5. They're referring to the original song here, as this song is Keiki's "Fanfare" (theme), as all touhou characters have musical themes.
    https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UoQLGz07vDk
    I think the slowed version is easier to 'musically understand' but that's just my take.
    https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kEpBEILwz_A     ↩
  6. "To go beyond a boundary" (My first thought when I see it is 'goku going super saiyan', if that helps)
    In my interpretation here I think it refers to reaching the final stage/beating it     ↩
  7. 描けない音 (kakenai oto) literally means
    "A sound/noise that cannot be drawn/paint/sketched/depicted/pictured in one's mind/give form"
    That is to say, a "Sound that one can 'see' and thus be able to depict."     ↩
  8. A play on the original song's title of "Entrusting this world to idols" but instead 
    "Entrust fantasy to this sound"     ↩
  9. Imagine telling someone you found something of _serious spiritual consideration_ from a video game about girls shooting laser beams at each other. ↩
  10. Speaking as someone who understands what the Author is trying to say and more or less agrees, I don't "believe" Keiki is "real/the real truth".... ↩
  11. But after having seen the 'Idola' that the song creator is referring to, I, too, felt that I had a sense of innocence stripped away from me from understanding it, that I was more of an 'adult' for having understood what I had seen from this "Idola" ↩
  12. Keiki embodies the idea that one should create and celebrate the 'gift that she gave the humans of the animal realm' -- the ability to imagine and take the depths of our imagination and turn it into something real with our own two hands. To 'give birth' to 'things' imbued with ideas and values because we say and agree that they have them.
    ....She does this by existing in the context that she does. She's also not an 'idola' one would want to mindlessly follow, as the rest of the game context implies.
    She has "one word" that she says in her portrait sprite in game:
    "Create!" (in English)
    Here, the writer is saying 'Oh, Idola, I have nothing to show but excuses'     ↩

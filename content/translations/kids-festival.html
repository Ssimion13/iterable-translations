Kid’s Festival – Innocent Treasures

A song ZUN wrote in one of his early albums. It is the only Touhou song with lyrics accompanying it. It is often referred to as ZUN’s theme, but I think it would be better to call this song the theme of Touhou itself.

The inspiration for Touhou in general comes from ZUN’s memories of the fireworks during the festivals in his hometown and the childlike wonder that comes along with it. This song carries the wonder of that feeling greatly.

The song seemingly intentionally leaves out key words (mostly verbs), as if inviting the listener to figure out what is missing in these gaps.

A song of a person looking at the real world and how it’s built on falsehoods and on false history, false assumptions, illusions that we all mutually agree upon that maintain our society….
A world that proudly claims the accuracy of it’s history and cold hard rational truth as the presumed bedrock of our world, sacrificing any sentimentality in the process.

The song does not make a statement either which way on these things other than what’s said above, but the questions it poses are clear as day:

Is the real world really any more or less illusionary, having replaced the false foundation our ancestors created in religion and spiritualism with what we have replaced it with?

We’ll know when we wake from the dream.

Translator’s notes

  1. Yume tagae is what one says when they have a bad dream to ward it away when they wake up. 
    We used to have a word like that, but I can't think of it.
    In this context, it refers to how in old folktale stories, the gods (or Buddha) would tell the future to a character in a dream, so they would say this as if to say "don't let this dream come true!"     ↩
  2. This sentence is marked as the object that will be affected by a verb, but the verb is never stated. ↩
  3. Lit. "A tower built on sand", something that lacks a strong foundation. ↩

    Once, Zhuang Zhou dreamed he was a butterfly, a butterfly flitting and fluttering about, happy with himself and doing as he pleased. He didn't know that he was Zhuang Zhou.
    俄然覺，則蘧蘧然周也。不知周之夢為胡蝶與，胡蝶之夢為周與。周與胡蝶，則必有分矣。此之謂物化。
    Suddenly he woke up and there he was, solid and unmistakable Zhuang Zhou. But he didn't know if he was Zhuang Zhou who had dreamt he was a butterfly, or a butterfly dreaming that he was Zhuang Zhou. Between Zhuang Zhou and the butterfly there must be some distinction!
